OHLC Projection Levels

Anchors to each period's open and projects two statistical levels around it: how far price usually wicks against the move, and how far it usually expands with it — measured across thousands of periods and rescaled to today's volatility.

How to installRead the source ↗
Two levels, four statistics

Up and down are not mirror images, so they aren't modelled as one.

The indicator tracks +M, −M, +D and −D separately. Nothing is derived by flipping a sign.

M

Manipulation

The counter-trend excursion — the liquidity grab before the move. Drawn at the median by default, so half of periods wick further and half don't.

D

Distribution

The in-trend expansion — where profit gets taken. Drawn at the 80th percentile by default: only one period in five extends past it.

Why "adaptive"

Six things that separate this from averaging raw percentages.

  1. Volatility normalisation

    Raw percentages mix regimes — 2011 BTC wicked over 10% a day, 2024 BTC wicks around 2%. Averaging them describes the regime mix, not tomorrow. In Log + ATR mode each period is stored as its log excursion divided by the ATR% prevailing at the time, then projected with today's ATR%. This is what makes a 5,000-period sample usable rather than merely large.

  2. Asymmetry

    Four separate distributions instead of one number mirrored across the open.

  3. Percentiles, not averages

    Mean and median answer "what is typical". A percentile answers "only 20% of periods extend past here" — a target with a stated probability.

  4. Recency weighting

    An optional exponential half-life, so recent periods dominate without discarding the tail. It trims the effective sample to about six half-lives, since older data then carries under 2% weight.

  5. Context conditioning

    Restrict the sample to periods sharing today's context — same weekday, similar prior-period range, similar opening gap. Range expansion clusters, so this is not cosmetic. It also splits the sample, so watch the n column.

  6. Honest sampling

    Standard errors beside every ratio, an amber table below your minimum sample, and reach/hold rates measured strictly walk-forward: each period judged only against estimates built from periods before it.

Key inputs

What to change, and what it does.

InputDefaultEffect
Projection timeframe1D4H, 1D, 1W, 1M or 1Y. Run the chart at or below it.
Sample size2000Rolling window of completed periods behind every statistic, reach and hold rates included.
Excursion measureLog + ATRPercent, log, or log normalised by prevailing ATR% — the recommended setting.
Manipulation percentile50Median wick. Read it as: exceeded by (100 − p)% of periods.
Distribution percentile80Only 20% of periods extend beyond this level.
Unconditional modeOffOff samples manipulation from periods that closed against that side. On lets every period contribute both excursions, removing a dependency on a close you don't know at the open.
Condition sample onNoneWeekday, prior-range tercile, or gap direction.
Minimum sample30Below it the table turns amber and reach/hold stays in burn-in. A 1Y projection will often sit under it permanently.
Data sources

Works on any exchange with zero setup.

By default the statistics come from your own chart symbol, requested at the projection timeframe — identical behaviour on OKX, Kraken, MEXC, Coinbase or Bitstamp. Where a listing is too young, override with a deeper feed and pool up to two more.

  • BITSTAMP:BTCUSD — 2011 onward
  • KRAKEN:BTCUSD — 2013 onward
  • COINBASE:BTCUSD — 2015 onward, or the CRYPTO:BTCUSD index

Ratios are dimensionless or percentage-based, so whichever feed supplies them they are always applied to your chart symbol's open.

Performance

Every enabled feed and every extra confluence timeframe costs a request.security call, and Pine executes all of them whether or not you use the result. If load time suffers, turn off pooling and extra timeframes first.

Chart timeframe

Run this at or below the projection timeframe — view 5m, 15m or 1H while projecting 1D or 1W.
