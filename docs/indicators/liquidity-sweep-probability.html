Liquidity Sweep Probability

Previous day, week, month and year highs and lows — each carrying the share of history it was actually swept, how often that sweep reversed, and where price went next.

How to installRead the source ↗

“The previous day's high has been swept on 63% of days over 4,812 days, and when it was, price reached Target 1 on 41% of them.”

Three numbers per level

Not "here is a level" — "here is what this level has done".

StatisticThe question it answers
Sweep probabilityHow often price traded through that level during the following period. Wicks count — liquidity taken is liquidity taken, whether or not the candle closed beyond it.
Reversal rateOf the times it was swept, how often price closed back inside. High means a classic stop hunt; low means the level breaks and price runs.
Recent vs all-timeThe same probability over the last N periods only, printed beside the lifetime figure. A wide gap means this level's behaviour has changed.
Never mirrored

Highs and lows are measured separately. Buy-side and sell-side liquidity do not get taken at the same rate, and no figure here is produced by flipping the sign of another.

Amber means thin

Below your minimum sample the number prints amber. The yearly timeframe will sit under it on almost every crypto asset — that is the honest answer, not a bug.

Targets

Run it twice and let the asset tell you which way sweeps resolve.

Each mode carries its own historical hit rate, so adding the indicator once per mode answers directly whether sweeps on this market more often continue or reverse.

ModePremiseWhere Target 1 lands
ContinuationThe sweep keeps going. Same side as the liquidity taken: sweep a high, aim for the high of the first earlier candle whose high is above the swept candle's.
ReversalThe sweep is a stop hunt and price turns. Far side of the swept candle: sweep a high, aim for the low of the first earlier candle whose low is below the swept candle's.

Why the search walks back

Both modes start two periods back and keep walking until a candle genuinely extends beyond the swept one. Without that condition a target can land inside range price has already traded through, which makes it meaningless.

Target 2 is nested inside Target 1

T2 is the first candle beyond T1 in the same direction. It always sits further out, so reaching it means T1 was passed on the way and its hit rate can never exceed T1's. The gap between them is the useful part.

When a target is missing

  • A price in the table but no line on the chart — the level hasn't been swept yet. Targets stay hidden until their level is taken, unless you turn on "show targets before the sweep".
  • The column reads none — no earlier candle within the search depth extends beyond the swept one. Routine in continuation mode near highs: an upside target needs an earlier candle with a higher high, and an asset printing new highs has none nearby. Raise the search depth to reach the previous major high.

When nothing qualifies, that period is excluded from the target statistics rather than counted as a miss.

An honest limitation

A single higher-timeframe candle records a high and a low but not their order. So the historical figure is strictly "this period swept the level and traded to the target", which slightly overstates "reached the target after sweeping". Live tracking on your chart does respect the order — a target only counts once its level has already been taken — so the live marker is stricter than the historical percentage printed beside it.

On the chart

Every line starts at the candle that made it.

A level begins at its source period; a target begins at whichever earlier period produced it. Each carries a price label, and taken levels are marked SWEPT rather than deleted — a swept level is still information, because it tells you the liquidity there is gone.

  • Statistics come from the chart symbol's own full history, per timeframe
  • Nothing repaints — only completed periods are ever read
  • Alerts fire on sweeps and on target hits
Chart timeframe

Run this on a timeframe finer than the levels you display. A weekly chart cannot resolve daily sweeps.
