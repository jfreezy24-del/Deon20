The method

One sequence, measured from six angles

The tools look different on a chart because they answer different questions. Read in order, they are a single workflow: find the liquidity, size the range, wait for the turn, and know where you are wrong.

See the toolsGet started

  1. Where is the liquidity, and how often does it get taken?

    Previous-period highs and lows are where stops and pending orders accumulate. Liquidity Sweep Probability marks them and puts a measured share on each: swept on 63% of days, reversed on 41% of those.

  2. How far does this market usually travel from the open?

    OHLC Projection Levels anchors to the period open and projects two distributions — the counter-trend wick and the in-trend expansion — at a percentile you choose, so a target arrives with a probability attached.

  3. Has the sweep actually turned?

    STATIC MODEL waits for the higher-timeframe candle to take the prior candle's extreme and close back inside, then looks for a change in state of delivery to confirm. Unicorn takes the same sweep into a breaker, and prefers the case where a fair value gap overlaps it.

  4. Where does it go, and when do I stop caring?

    Both models project targets from the structure that produced the setup — the swept candle's equilibrium and far side, or R multiples off the confirmation close — and both place invalidation at the wick that did the sweeping.

Vocabulary

The words these tools use, and exactly what they mean here.

TermDefinition
SweepPrice trades through a level, wick included. Liquidity taken is liquidity taken, whether or not the candle closed beyond it.
C1 / C2The prior higher-timeframe candle and the current one. A C2 setup is the current candle sweeping C1's high or low and trading back inside it.
CISDChange in state of delivery — the level whose break says the previous delivery direction has ended. STATIC MODEL uses it as confirmation.
BreakerThe candle whose structure gets broken after a sweep. Price returning into it is the entry zone.
UnicornA breaker with a fair value gap sitting on top of it — two reasons for the same zone.
Manipulation (M)The excursion against the period's eventual direction: the wick that hunts stops before the move.
Distribution (D)The excursion with the period's direction: the expansion you take profit into.
Equilibrium (EQ)The 50% level of a range. A swept candle's EQ is the first target in STATIC MODEL.
FTFCFull timeframe continuity — 4H, daily, weekly and monthly candles all on the same side of their opens.
Walk-forwardEach period judged only against estimates built from periods before it. The only honest way to state a hit rate.

House rules

Constraints every one of these scripts is held to.

Nothing repaints

Every statistic is built from completed, confirmed periods. A number that changes after the fact is not a number.

Sample size is stated

Tables carry an n column and turn amber under your minimum. A thin sample says so rather than averaging into false confidence.

No mirrored statistics

Buy-side and sell-side are measured separately, always. No figure is produced by flipping the sign of another.

Levels are anchored to their source

A line starts at the candle that produced it, so you can always see where a level came from.

Limitations are written down

Where a measurement overstates its case, the script's own header says so — and so does this site.

One file, no dependencies

Every indicator is a single self-contained Pine script. Nothing to build, no libraries to trust.
