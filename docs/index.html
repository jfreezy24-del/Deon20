Why these are different

Most indicators draw a level. These tell you how often it holds.

%

Measured, not drawn

Every probability is counted from the symbol's own completed history — thousands of periods, walked forward, never fitted after the fact.

n

Honest about the sample

Tables carry an n column and turn amber below your minimum. A yearly stat on a young asset says so instead of pretending.

↕

Up and down measured apart

Buy-side and sell-side liquidity do not get taken at the same rate, so no statistic is ever produced by flipping the sign of another.

How they fit together

Four questions, in the order you'd actually ask them.

  1. Where is the liquidity?

    Previous-period highs and lows are where stops sit. Liquidity Sweep Probability marks them and prices each one.

  2. How far does this market travel?

    OHLC Projection Levels anchors to the open and projects the wick and the expansion at a percentile you pick.

  3. Has the sweep turned?

    STATIC MODEL waits for the reclaim and a change in state of delivery. Unicorn takes the same sweep into a breaker.

  4. Where does it go, and when do I stop caring?

    Both models project targets from the structure that made the setup, and put invalidation at the wick that swept.

Statistics you can argue with

A probability without a sample size is a decoration.

Every reach and hold rate is measured walk-forward — each period judged only against estimates built from the periods before it. Standard errors sit beside the ratios. Sub-threshold samples go amber rather than quietly averaging into confidence they haven't earned.

  • Volatility-normalised history, so a 2011 regime doesn't set today's target
  • Optional recency weighting and context conditioning by weekday, prior range or gap
  • Documented limitations — including the one place the target stat overstates itself

Questions

Before you install

Do any of them repaint?
No. Every statistic is built from completed, confirmed periods only, and levels are anchored to the candle that produced them. What you see on a historical bar is what the indicator showed at the time.
Do I need a paid TradingView plan?
Not for the indicators themselves. The heavier ones request higher-timeframe data per feed, and each pooled feed or extra confluence timeframe costs a request — if load time suffers on a free plan, turn off pooling first. Running several of these on one chart at once is where plan limits start to matter.
Which one should I start with?
Liquidity Sweep Probability. It needs no configuration, works on any symbol, and immediately tells you something you did not know: how often this market actually takes the level you were about to trade.
Crypto or stocks?
Both. The sweep and model indicators are market-agnostic and default to your chart symbol's own history. OHLC Projection ships in two editions because equities gap overnight and crypto does not, and that changes what the sample should be conditioned on.
Is this financial advice?
No. These are measurement tools. They describe how a market has behaved, which is a record of the past, not a forecast — and every figure is printed with the sample size behind it precisely so you can judge how much weight it deserves.
