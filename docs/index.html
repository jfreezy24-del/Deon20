Six Pine Script indicators · one mobile scanner

Every level here was measured, not drawn.

This suite is built on one observation: price reaches for resting liquidity, takes it, and then either keeps going or turns. Each tool measures a different part of that sequence — where the liquidity sits, how often it actually gets taken, what confirms the turn, and how far the move that follows tends to travel.

What they have in common

Design rules
  • Nothing repaints. Every statistic is built from completed, confirmed periods. A number that changes after the fact is not a number.
  • Sample size is stated, not hidden. Tables carry an n column and turn amber under the minimum you set. A yearly sample on a five-year-old asset says so.
  • Up and down are measured separately. Buy-side and sell-side liquidity are never assumed to be mirror images, and no statistic is derived by flipping the sign of another.
  • Levels are anchored to their source. A line starts at the candle that produced it, so you can always see where a level came from.
  • Limitations are written down. Where a measurement overstates its case — a daily candle records a high and a low but not their order — the docs say so in the file.

Overview

The method behind all seven

The tools look different on a chart because they answer different questions. Read in order, they are one workflow.

Four questions, in order

Workflow
  1. Where is the liquidity, and how often does it get taken? Previous-period highs and lows are where stops and pending orders accumulate. Liquidity Sweep Probability marks them and puts a measured share on each: swept on 63% of days, reversed on 41% of those.
  2. How far does this market usually travel from the open? OHLC Projection Levels anchors to the period open and projects two distributions — the counter-trend wick and the in-trend expansion — at a percentile you choose, so a target arrives with a probability attached.
  3. Has the sweep actually turned? STATIC MODEL waits for the higher-timeframe candle to take the prior candle's extreme and close back inside, then looks for a change in state of delivery to confirm. Unicorn takes the same sweep into a breaker, and prefers the case where a fair value gap overlaps it.
  4. Where does it go, and when do I stop caring? Both models project targets from the structure that produced the setup — the swept candle's equilibrium and far side, or R multiples off the confirmation close — and both place invalidation at the wick that did the sweeping.

STATIC MODEL

The higher-timeframe candle takes the previous candle's low, closes back above it, and the delivery flips. This draws that sequence end to end — sweep, confirmation, targets, invalidation — and keeps the record of every setup it has printed.

Pine v6 Overlay Auto or manual HTF Alerts on CISD break 1,622 lines
static-model.pine ↗

The setup

C2

Call the previous higher-timeframe candle C1 and the live one C2. A setup exists when C2 sweeps C1's low or high and trades back inside C1's range.

That is the whole entry premise: someone's stops were filled outside the range and price did not stay there. The indicator marks the sweep, draws the invalidation at the sweep wick, and waits for a change in state of delivery to confirm the turn. If price breaks back through the invalidation the setup is marked XC2 and stops being live — the marker and the breached line stay on the chart so the record is honest.

Targets

Projected from C2's sweep of C1, not from a round number: 50% is C1's equilibrium, 100% is the opposite side of C1 — the full range of the candle that was swept. Both lines extend until they are hit or the setup expires.

Confirmation: CISD

Entry

CISD levels are tracked in two modes. Confirmed waits for the closed break; Hybrid surfaces the level earlier. Each level carries a visible state — pending, faded, confirmed — with its own colour, so you can see a level maturing rather than only its final verdict. Optional alerts fire when price breaks the current +CISD or -CISD level.

STATIC MODEL — Screener

The same C2 logic pointed at up to 40 symbols at once, from a single chart, with one alert per bar no matter how many symbols fire.

Pine v6 Up to 40 symbols Forming + confirmed One alert per chart bar
static-model-screener.pine ↗

Two stages

States
StageConditionCaveat
Forming The live HTF candle has swept the prior candle's low or high and is trading back inside. Can still un-form before the candle closes. This is the early warning, not the signal.
Confirmed The HTF candle closed back above the swept low (buy) or below the swept high (sell). A valid C2. For CISD confirmation and the entry itself, open the full indicator on that symbol.

Alert design

Notifications

Every signal produced by one chart bar is bundled into a single alert() message — one line per signal — so the alert fires at most once per bar and stays comfortably under TradingView's trigger-frequency limits. Each stage also alerts at most once per HTF candle per symbol, so a setup that flickers in and out of "forming" does not spam you.

Setup

3 steps
  1. Add the screener to a 1–5 minute chart of any liquid symbol. The chart timeframe must be at or below the scan timeframe — it raises a runtime error otherwise — and a fast chart gives the fastest updates.
  2. Paste your symbol list into the text area, comma or newline separated, and set the scan timeframe (defaults to 4H).
  3. Create one alert on the indicator with condition Any alert() function call and push notifications enabled.
Defaults ship with a mixed crypto and US equity list — BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, the Mag 7, SPY and QQQ. Forming and confirmed alerts can be toggled independently, and the bias filter narrows the scan to one direction.

Unicorn Model

Liquidity sweep into a breaker — and the "unicorn" case where a fair value gap overlaps that breaker, giving the same zone two independent reasons to matter. Ships with the trade management, not just the marking.

Pine v6 Breaker / Unicorn / both Auto break-even Daily trade limits 2,145 lines
unicorn-model.pine ↗

The model

Zone

A sweep takes liquidity; the candle that then gets broken becomes the breaker; price returning into it is the entry. When a fair value gap sits over that breaker, the overlap is the unicorn — and you can restrict the indicator to only those.

Setups can be shown while still potential, so you see the zone before it activates. Invalidated setups are either ghosted in a faded colour or discarded entirely, your choice. Invalidation defaults to the model's own structural level, or optionally the manipulation extreme — the swing that did the sweeping.

Where liquidity comes from

Sources

Three configurable sources run at once — by default 1H, 4H and daily — and each can draw its liquidity from OHLC levels, swing points, or both. Mapping is automatic or manual. On top of that, session liquidity tracks the highs and lows of Asia, London, New York AM and New York PM, each with an editable window.

OHLC Projection Levels — Adaptive

Anchors to each period's open and projects two statistical levels around it: how far price usually wicks against the move, and how far it usually expands with it — measured across thousands of periods and rescaled to today's volatility.

Pine v6 4H · 1D · 1W · 1M · 1Y Up to 5,000 periods Walk-forward reach/hold
ohlc-projection-levels.pine ↗

Two levels, four statistics

M / D

Manipulation (M) is the counter-trend excursion — the liquidity grab before the move. Distribution (D) is the in-trend expansion — where profit gets taken. Up and down are tracked separately, so the model carries four statistics (+M, −M, +D, −D) rather than one number mirrored across the open.

Why "adaptive"

Six ideas
  1. Volatility normalisation. Raw percentages mix regimes — 2011 BTC wicked over 10% a day, 2024 BTC wicks around 2%. Averaging them describes the regime mix, not tomorrow. In Log + ATR mode each period is stored as its log excursion divided by the ATR% prevailing at the time, then projected with today's ATR%. This is what makes a 5,000-period sample usable rather than merely large.
  2. Asymmetry. Four separate distributions, never a sign flip.
  3. Percentiles. Mean and median answer "what is typical". A percentile answers "only 20% of periods extend past here" — a target with a stated probability.
  4. Recency weighting. Optional exponential half-life, so recent periods dominate without discarding the tail.
  5. Context conditioning. Restrict the sample to periods that share today's context — same weekday, similar prior-period range, similar opening gap. Range expansion clusters, so this is not cosmetic.
  6. Honest sampling. Standard errors beside every ratio, the table turns amber below your minimum sample, and reach/hold rates are measured strictly walk-forward: each period judged only against estimates built from periods before it.

Data sources

Crypto

By default the statistics come from your own chart symbol, requested at the projection timeframe — so it works identically on OKX, Kraken, MEXC, Coinbase or Bitstamp with zero setup. Where a listing is too young, override with a deeper feed and pool up to two more: BITSTAMP:BTCUSD (2011+), KRAKEN:BTCUSD (2013+), COINBASE:BTCUSD (2015+), or the CRYPTO:BTCUSD index. Ratios are dimensionless or percentage-based, so whichever feed supplies them they are always applied to your chart symbol's open.

Performance. Every enabled feed and every extra confluence timeframe costs a request.security call, and Pine executes all of them whether or not you use the result. If load time suffers, turn off pooling and extra timeframes first.

Read this first

The statistical model is identical to the crypto edition — manipulation and distribution percentiles, ATR normalisation, recency weighting, conditioning, walk-forward reach and hold. That page covers the engine. What follows is only what differs for equities.

What changes for stocks

Deltas
  • Overnight gaps are part of the sample. A gap straight through a level counts as reached, exactly as it trades. The Gap direction conditioning mode exists for this reason and is worth trying here even though it ships off — an up-gap open is a different animal from a flat one.
  • Sessions differ between feeds. Index, futures and CFD feeds run different hours than cash equities, so their excursion ratios are comparable but not identical. Pooling smooths that, and ATR normalisation makes it far less damaging.
  • Keep split adjustment on in TradingView, so decades-old percentages stay meaningful.
  • Prefer 1D and above. The 4H option aligns to exchange time and sits awkwardly against a 6.5-hour cash session.

Liquidity Sweep Probability

Previous day, week, month and year highs and lows — each carrying the share of history it was actually swept, how often that sweep reversed, and where price went afterwards.

Pine v6 D · W · M · Y Two target modes No repaint
liquidity-sweep-probability.pine ↗

“The previous day's high has been swept on 63% of days over 4,812 days, and when it was, price reached Target 1 on 41% of them.”

Three numbers per level

Statistics
StatisticQuestion it answers
Sweep probabilityHow often price traded through that level during the following period. Wicks count — liquidity taken is liquidity taken.
Reversal rateOf the times it was swept, how often price closed back inside. High means a classic stop hunt; low means the level breaks and price runs.
Recent vs all-timeThe same sweep probability over the last N periods only. A wide gap means the behaviour of that level has changed.

Highs and lows are measured separately, never mirrored — buy-side and sell-side liquidity do not get taken at the same rate. Below your minimum sample the figure prints in amber; the yearly timeframe will sit under it on almost every crypto asset, which is honest rather than broken.

Targets: continuation or reversal

T1 / T2
ModePremiseWhere T1 lands
Continuation The sweep keeps going. Same side as the liquidity taken: sweep a high, aim for the high of the first earlier candle whose high is above the swept candle's.
Reversal The sweep is a stop hunt and price turns. Far side of the swept candle: sweep a high, aim for the low of the first earlier candle whose low is below the swept candle's.

Both modes start two periods back and walk further until a candle genuinely extends beyond the swept one — without that condition a target can land inside range price has already traded through, which makes it meaningless. Target 2 is the next rung of the same ladder: the first candle beyond T1 in the same direction. Because T2 always sits further out on the same side, reaching it means T1 was passed on the way, so the two hit rates are naturally nested and T2 can never exceed T1. The gap between them is the useful part — it tells you how much of the time a move that reaches T1 keeps going.

Each mode carries its own historical hit rate, so running the indicator twice — once per mode — tells you directly whether sweeps on this asset more often continue or reverse.

When a target is missing

Two cases
  • The table shows a price but no line is drawn. The level simply has not been swept yet — targets stay hidden until their level is taken, unless you turn on "show targets before the sweep".
  • The column reads none. No earlier candle within the search depth extends beyond the swept one. Routine in continuation mode near highs: an upside target needs an earlier candle with a higher high, and an asset printing new highs has none nearby. Raise the search depth to reach the previous major high.

When nothing qualifies, that period is excluded from the target statistics rather than counted as a miss.

An honest limitation. A single higher-timeframe candle records a high and a low but not their order. So the historical figure is strictly "this period swept the level and traded to the target", which slightly overstates "reached the target after sweeping". Live tracking on your chart does respect the order — a target only counts once its level has already been taken — so the live marker is stricter than the historical percentage printed beside it.

On the chart

Display

Every line is anchored to the candle that created it, so a level starts at its source period and a target starts at whichever earlier period produced it. Each carries a price label and taken levels are marked SWEPT. Levels are coloured by timeframe — day, week, month, year — while targets keep their own fixed colours so the two never blur together. Swept levels can be dimmed or hidden; the source candle's range can be shaded. Alerts fire on sweeps and target hits.

Run this on a chart timeframe finer than the levels you display. A weekly chart cannot resolve daily sweeps.

strat-scanner/ ↗

Potential setups, not finished ones

Scanner

Most scanners find sequences that have already completed. This one finds unresolved X-1-? forks where the "?" is still to come — and lays out both branches before either happens.

The classic case is a directional bar followed by an inside bar (2u-1-?): break the inside bar's high and the move continues, break its low and it reverses. Inside-bar forks are flagged as compression — tightest risk, genuinely undecided direction. If the forming candle has already taken a trigger out, the signal is marked TRIGGERED rather than POTENTIAL.

Why it is validPlain English: the candle sequence, the trapped participants, the continuity picture, the magnitude logic.

Algo Alerts mode

Confirmed only

The second mode drops the personal watchlist and watches a fixed universe — the Mag 7 plus the most liquid US ETFs (SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA, SMH, the sector SPDRs, GLD, TLT; no penny stocks) — showing confirmed triggers only on daily, weekly and monthly structure. Each card is a complete plan, and when a sector ETF fires it lists same-direction setups in its heavy constituents; when a stock fires it shows the sector ETFs confirming the move. Expect a handful of names on a good day and zero on most.

Alerts with the app closed

Server

The same engine runs headless on GitHub Actions and pushes to your phone through ntfy.sh — free, no account. Set one repository secret (NTFY_TOPIC), subscribe to that topic in the ntfy app, and the scan runs every 15 minutes during US market hours. Add ALERT_EMAIL and copies are forwarded to your inbox through ntfy's email bridge — no SMTP setup.

Weekly crypto ladders

Accumulation

Accumulation only — no trade triggers. Each asset gets a standing ladder whose rungs are structure rather than round numbers: prior week low, weekly pivot low, prior month low, monthly pivot low. Size is skewed by tier — majors front-load the shallow rungs, high-beta names back-load the deep ones — rungs are never more than 35–65% below price, and each ladder states its average fill if the whole thing fills. Assets are ordered closest-to-filling first, and each carries a stance: ACCUMULATE, NEUTRAL or DEFENSIVE.

Does it actually work?

Validation

The repo carries the tooling to answer that rather than assert it: a forward-walk outcome resolver, a record of every published signal, historical replay with no look-ahead, an exit-policy grid with walk-forward validation, regime labelling from a benchmark, and a target-quality diagnostic asking whether Target 1 is real structure. Entry refinement uses 60-minute bars for timing only — never for selection.
