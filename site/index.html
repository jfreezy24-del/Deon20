Measured, not drawn

A level without a hit rate is a guess

Anyone can mark yesterday’s high. These tools tell you how often that line has actually been traded through, how often price closed back inside it, and how far it went when it didn’t.

Walk-forward

Scored only against its own past

Reach and hold rates judge each period using estimates built strictly from periods before it. No statistic on screen has seen the bar it is being graded on.

Honest sampling

The sample size is on the table

Standard errors sit beside every ratio, and the table turns amber the moment a level drops below the minimum sample you set. Thin data announces itself.

Indicator 01

Liquidity Sweep Probability

The previous period’s high and low is where resting stops pile up. This marks those levels for the day, week, month and year, and puts a historically measured probability on each one — the previous day’s high has been swept on 63% of days over 4,812 days, and when it was, price reached Target 1 on 41% of them.

Sweep
Wick counts. Liquidity taken is liquidity taken, whether or not price closed beyond.
Reversal
High reversal rate = classic stop hunt. Low = the level breaks and price runs.
Targets
Two modes. Continuation targets the same side as the swept liquidity; Reversal targets the far side of the swept candle. Each carries its own hit rate, so running both tells you which this asset does.
Nesting
T2 always sits beyond T1, so T2 hit can never exceed T1 hit. The gap between the two columns is how often a move that reaches T1 keeps going.

Indicator 02 — crypto & stocks editions

OHLC Projection Levels — Adaptive

Anchors to each period’s open and projects two families of level around it: manipulation, the counter-trend excursion where stops get taken, and distribution, the in-trend expansion where targets sit.

Adaptive
Raw percentages mix regimes — 2011 BTC wicked 10% a day, 2024 BTC wicks 2%. Each period is stored as excursion ÷ the ATR% prevailing at the time, then projected with today’s ATR%. That is what makes a 5,000-period sample usable rather than merely large.
Asymmetric
Four separate statistics (+M -M +D -D), never one number mirrored.
Conditioned
Optionally restrict the sample to periods sharing today’s context — same weekday, similar prior range, similar opening gap. Range expansion clusters, so this is not cosmetic.
Both-side
The whipsaw rate: how often both manipulation levels were tagged in the same period. It is the rate at which this model’s own premise fails, and it is on the table.

Indicator 03

Static Model

The higher-timeframe candle sweeps the prior candle’s low and closes back above it — a C2. From there the model projects the swept candle’s equilibrium and its far side as targets, and waits on a change in state of delivery to confirm the entry.

Context
HTF open and HTF EQ drawn as lines, with the trading zone between them shaded.
CISD
+CISD / -CISD levels in two modes — Confirmed and Hybrid — with pending, faded and confirmed states each carrying their own colour.
Targets
TGT 50% is the swept candle’s equilibrium; TGT 100% is its opposite side. Lines extend until hit, or until the setup expires or invalidates.
Extras
Market-structure markers, first-FVG labelling, C4 continuation, deviation counts and a bias filter that suppresses one side entirely.

Indicator 04

Static Model Screener

One chart, forty symbols. It watches every one of them for the same C2 the main indicator draws, in two stages: forming, while the live candle has swept and is trading back inside, and confirmed, once it closes back through.

Setup
Add it to a 1–5m chart, create one alert with condition Any alert() function call, enable push. That is the whole configuration.
Bundling
Every signal from a chart bar is packed into a single alert message, one line each — so it fires at most once per bar and stays inside TradingView’s trigger-frequency limits.
De-duplication
Alerts are written on the confirmed tick only. Realtime ticks roll back state, so mid-bar writes would let the same signal re-fire on every tick.
Forming states
C2 Buy · C2 Sell · Sweep ↑ · Sweep ↓ — a forming signal can still un-form before the close, and the table says so.

Indicator 05

Unicorn Model

A sweep of resting liquidity, then the breaker it leaves behind. When the displacement away from that breaker also leaves a fair value gap on top of it, the overlap is the unicorn — and only that overlap is traded.

Modes
Breaker, Unicorn, or both. In Unicorn mode a setup is only shown once an FVG actually overlaps the breaker.
Risk
Invalidation at the sweep (or the swing behind it), R multiples projected from the entry, and a break-even trigger that arms at the 50% mark and moves the stop for you.
Context
Session filters, higher-timeframe FVG overlay, and SMT divergence against a paired symbol.
Ledger
A month-by-month table of taken, won, lost, broken even and win rate — from the setups the indicator itself marked, on your chart, in front of you.

App 06 — React Native / Expo

Strat Scanner

Every other Strat screener shows you sequences that already completed. This one scans for the unresolved fork — the setups where the next bar is still the question mark — and lays out both branches before either happens.

The fork
The last completed candle is the actionable bar; its high and low are the triggers. Break one way and it is a continuation, the other and it is a reversal. Both are priced up front.
Compression
Inside-bar forks (X-1-?) are flagged separately — tightest risk, genuinely undecided direction.
Continuity
The 4H / D / W / M strip checks each live candle against its open. Alignment adds confidence, conflict subtracts it, and the arithmetic is printed on the card.
Data
No API keys. Yahoo’s public chart API covers stocks, ETFs, indices, crypto, forex and futures; 1H bars are aggregated into session-anchored 4H buckets because Yahoo has no native 4H.

Method

How a number gets onto the chart

The same four stages run behind every level in the suite. They are stated here because the order is what makes the figure trustworthy — and because most of the ways to get this wrong look identical from the outside.

STAGE 01

Pull the deepest history there is

Statistics come from the chart symbol’s own higher-timeframe history, requested per timeframe. Optionally override with a longer-history feed and pool up to two more — ratios are dimensionless, so whichever provider supplies them they land on your symbol’s open.

STAGE 02

Normalise away the regime

Each period is stored relative to the volatility prevailing when it happened, not as a raw percentage. Averaging raw percentages describes the regime mix in your sample; it does not describe tomorrow.

STAGE 03

Score it walk-forward

Reach and hold rates judge each period only against estimates built from periods before it, over the same rolling window as the ratios. A statistic that has seen its own answer is not a statistic.

STAGE 04

Publish the uncertainty with it

Standard error beside every ratio, sample count in its own column, and an amber table the moment a level falls under the minimum sample. The warning is part of the output, not a footnote.

One limitation stated plainly

A single higher-timeframe candle records a high and a low but not the order they arrived in. So the historical target figure is strictly “this period swept the level and traded to the target”, which slightly overstates “reached the target after sweeping”. Live tracking on your chart does respect the order — a target only counts once its level has already been taken — so the live marker is stricter than the historical percentage printed beside it.

Get started

Four minutes to first level

TradingView — Pine Editor
  1. Open the Pine Editor and paste the indicator source.
  2. Hit Add to chart. Defaults use your chart symbol, so there is nothing to configure to see the first levels.
  3. Run it on a chart timeframe at or finer than the levels you are projecting — a weekly chart cannot resolve daily sweeps, and the table will tell you so with CHART > PROJ TF.
  4. For the screener: a 1–5m chart, one alert on Any alert() function call, push notifications on.
  5. For Strat Scanner: npm install, npx expo start, scan the QR with Expo Go.
